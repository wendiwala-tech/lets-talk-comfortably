CONVERSATION PRACTICE: QA AND USABILITY SESSION GUIDE
PBHLTH 182 | Group 103-3 | Internal organizer document

中文组织提示
这是一份待执行的测试计划，不是测试结果。目标约 5 名 Berkeley 学生，
每人预留约 15-20 分钟；允许跳过或提前结束。只测试网站是否易懂、易用，
不询问个人性经历、伴侣情况或健康信息。评分可以使用测试用的假设答案，
不需要反映真实感受。所有网站交互都应点选，不要求输入文字。
角色组合也只是练习设定，不询问或推断试用者的性别、性取向或实际关系。
四个主题、角色组合和三级辅助程度应独立选择；切换角色不应重置当前轮次。
以 P1-P5 匿名记录；所有结果栏留空，观察后再填。不录音、不录像，
不把计划人数写成已测试人数。组员 QA 不计入学生试用人数。

1. TEAM QA - COMPLETE BEFORE INVITING PARTICIPANTS

Site and stories
[ ] Four complete modules and all six public pages load for visitors who do
    not have the group's account. Campus resources open the intended pages.
[ ] Each story has six rounds. Choices affect subsequent exchanges; every
    intended ending is reachable. Feedback matches the selected choice.
[ ] Review, rewind, restart, and pause/resume work at every support level.
    Rewinding replaces later choices on the abandoned route. Pause/resume
    preserves the current round and any reply being reviewed.
[ ] A story ending where characters stop is a valid completion, distinct from
    the interface's Pause practice control. There is no required agreement.
[ ] Authored examples do not claim to be interviews, measured outcomes, or
    endorsements. Wording permits declining, pausing, and changing one's mind.

Characters and topic independence
[ ] Every topic supports all five practice profiles: woman + man with the
    woman as the learner's character; woman + man with the man as the learner's
    character; woman + woman; man + man; and gender-neutral roles.
[ ] A pairing selects characters, not a topic or support level. All profiles
    retain the same six-round choice graph and have access to every ending.
    A learner does not have to identify personally with the example characters.
[ ] Within each topic, check cast names/pronouns, setting, dialogue, feedback,
    conversation history, and ending. Both mixed-pair viewpoints reverse the
    learner/partner names correctly; same-gender roles work in every topic.
[ ] Check hero and module illustrations for each pairing. Every topic must
    depict the selected pair. Neutral roles use gender-neutral labels; artwork
    should not be treated as proof of a person's gender identity.
[ ] Change characters before starting, during a round, while reviewing a reply,
    and at an ending. The current round, pending reply, earlier choice route,
    support level, and completed-story count stay intact. Displayed character
    names and story setting update without retaining the old cast's wording.
[ ] Select a profile, visit another topic, and reload. The chosen pairing and
    mixed-pair viewpoint persist with the practice path. Clear path restores
    the default neutral roles and removes the saved practice settings.

Opening check-in and support levels
[ ] With test values, verify all opening ratings: 4/5 recommends Level 1;
    2/3 recommends Level 2; 1 recommends Level 3.
[ ] The rating is a subjective check-in about the example conversation, not
    a diagnosis or an ability test. Learners can choose any support level.
[ ] Change levels before starting, during a round, while reviewing a reply,
    after a rewind, and at an ending. Support changes should preserve the
    story position and current route unless a clear reset was requested.
[ ] Level 1 automatically shows coaching and offers an optional tap-to-choose
    warm-up. Across all six rounds, open the activity, try every option, and
    verify its response. The activity must not select a story branch by accident.
[ ] Level 2 makes help optional and discoverable. Opening a nudge does not
    advance a round, select dialogue, or reset the learner's progress.
[ ] Level 3 shows actual dialogue choices before their reasoning. After a
    choice, its reasoning is available; no writing or hidden grading occurs.
[ ] All levels retain the same complete branching story and access to all
    endings. Learners can always return to more support; nothing is locked.
[ ] No screen asks for typed input: no textarea, text field, contenteditable
    exercise, or forced sentence-writing step. All reflection is selectable.

Ending, self-report, and progression
[ ] Test lower, unchanged, and higher ending ratings, plus leaving it unanswered. Before/after
    values match the inputs. No display claims that the site caused change,
    diagnoses a problem, or treats a higher rating as a failure.
[ ] Without a baseline, no invented before/after comparison appears. Changing
    support does not overwrite a previous check-in value.
[ ] Ending reflection lets learners tap a skill they want to practice next.
    Trying every option gives appropriate feedback or a clear next step.
[ ] Finishing marks the intended completion. Reopening an ending, replaying,
    rewinding, or changing levels does not falsely add completed modules.
[ ] Repeating, advancing, or choosing more support remains optional.
    Celebration acknowledges practice or reflection, not persuading a partner
    to agree, picking a supposed correct ending, or reporting a lower rating.

Privacy and Clear path
[ ] Inspect the practice-related sessionStorage with invented test values.
    Only documented level, baseline, completed topics, relationship profile,
    and mixed-pair viewpoint persist (level, baseline, completed, relationship,
    mixedRole). These are practice settings, not a declaration of identity.
    No full conversation history, personal free text, or dialogue is stored.
[ ] Navigate across modules and reload. Only the promised path information
    remains. Story choices do not reappear as if the route were saved.
[ ] Clear path removes practice session data and resets the visible baseline,
    support/path state, character settings, and completion markers as described. Revisit another
    module and reload: old path data must not return.
[ ] Clear path does not unexpectedly change the separate appearance setting.
    Practice remains usable when browser storage is unavailable.
[ ] Privacy wording matches the actual storage scope. Do not promise browser
    erasure behavior that cannot be ensured.

Accessibility and playful interactions
[ ] Phone, desktop, and enlarged-text layouts have no clipped text, overlapping
    controls, sideways scrolling, or hidden choices.
[ ] Keyboard users can select pairings and viewpoints, ratings/levels, use warm-ups/help, select dialogue,
    continue, rewind, pause, and reach all ending and reflection options.
[ ] Focus moves predictably after changes. Selected pairings/viewpoints and ratings/levels have clear
    accessible states; important information does not depend on color or emoji.
[ ] Emoji have useful accompanying text or are decorative. Screen readers do
    not repeatedly announce decorative symbols as essential instructions.
[ ] Reduced-motion mode removes or reduces celebratory movement. Animation,
    sound, hover, and rapid reactions are never required to continue.

2. PARTICIPANT SCRIPT - USE THE SAME TASKS

Opening (read aloud):
"We are testing a class-project website, not your knowledge or communication
ability. Please use the example conversations; you do not need to share
personal experiences, relationships, or health information. You can use
made-up test answers for the ratings, skip a question, or stop whenever you
like. The character options are example settings, not questions about your
identity or actual relationships. May we take anonymous notes about how the
website works?"

Proceed only with agreement. Record no names, contact details, or personal
ratings. Do not explain navigation first. Record any assistance given.

Task 1 - Find a starting point:
"Choose a conversation you are comfortable viewing and any example character
pairing. If you pick woman + man, try choosing either character's viewpoint.
Explain whether the pairing changes which topics you can practice.
Try the opening check-in
with a test answer. Tell us what the recommended level means and whether you
can choose a different one. Choose a level to try."
Observe: Can the learner distinguish pairing, topic, viewpoint, and support
level? Are all topics visibly available regardless of pairing? Does the cast
and artwork match the selected example? Then check scale meaning,
recommendation, level selection, and whether levels
are interpreted as support options or a grade. Do not request a personal
explanation for a rating or pairing; record no personal identity or score.

Task 2 - Try support and complete a story:
"Begin the example story. Try the help or warm-up offered at your level.
After a couple of rounds, switch support levels and see what changes.
Try changing the characters while staying in the same story. Pause and resume
once. Continue to an ending if you feel comfortable."
Observe: Can the learner distinguish a warm-up from a dialogue choice and
Continue? Is feedback useful? Can they find optional help? Does Level 3 let
them choose first and inspect reasoning afterward? Do level switching and
character switching and pause/resume preserve the expected position? Does a
pending reply remain visible under the newly selected character names? Record the last round reached
and any help requested. Choosing a pause ending is not a task failure.

Task 3 - Interpret the ending:
"Try the optional ending check-in with a test answer, or skip it. Explain
what the before/after display and next-step suggestion mean. Select a skill
you might practice next, then choose whether to repeat or change level."
Observe: Do learners recognize a subjective report rather than proof of
improvement? Do they feel required to report less awkwardness, advance, or
reach a particular ending? Are reflection and next-step choices clear?
Record interpretations of the interface, not personal scores.

Task 4 - Explore another route:
"Review the conversation. Change an earlier choice and see what happens next.
What do you expect the website to keep or reset? You may stop before finishing
this second route."
Observe: Can they find Review your conversation, Change this choice, and Try
another ending? Does the later route get replaced as expected? Can they still
change support? Record only the routes/endings actually observed.

Task 5 - Find a resource:
"Open a campus resource. Based on its label, what information or support would
you expect to find there?"
Observe: Resource discoverability, destination, and misleading or broken links.

Task 6 - Understand and clear the path:
"Visit another conversation or reload. What has the website remembered?
Find Clear path, use it, and check what changed."
Observe: Can they distinguish saved path settings from unsaved story choices?
Does their chosen pairing/viewpoint remain available in the other topic?
Is Clear path understandable, and do old markers and character settings stay
cleared after navigation?

Closing questions:
1. Which instruction, level, activity, or control was hardest to understand?
2. What happened differently from what you expected?
3. What one change would make the practice easier or more useful?
Optional: "Did you feel pushed to advance or report less awkwardness? Was it
clear that you could choose more support or stop?"

Keep notes about the interface. If private information is volunteered, do not
record it; redirect to the example or controls. Thank the participant.

4. EVIDENCE FOR THE PRESENTATION

- Deployed URL, version, actual dates, and screenshots. Separate team QA from
  external usability sessions. Actual distinct participants: ____.
- Each task: independent ____ / observed ____; with help ____;
  not completed ____; skipped/stopped ____. Leave unobserved tasks blank.
- Actual coverage: topics ____, example profiles ____, mixed viewpoints ____,
  character switches ____, matching artwork ____, levels ____, warm-ups ____, pause/resume ____, check-in ____,
  selectable reflection ____, rewind ____, Clear path ____.
- Whether learners understood support levels and subjective check-ins, based
  on observed interface interpretations. Do not collect or report private scores.
- Whether learners understood that character pairing is independent of topic
  and level. Report only observed usability interpretations, not identities.
- Two or three specific findings and resulting changes. State whether each
  change was rechecked by teammates, participants, or neither.
- Reach, if measured: metric ____, count ____, dates ____, method ____.
  Keep page views, unique visitors, and usability participants separate.
  If unavailable, say "not measured"; do not invent zero.
- Limits: small convenience sample; optional before/after ratings do not
  establish causation, durable skill gains, behavior change, or health outcomes.
